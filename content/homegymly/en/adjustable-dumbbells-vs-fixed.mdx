The honest comparison nobody makes is not about price. It is about **seconds between sets**.

## The space and money case for adjustable

A single pair of adjustable dumbbells replaces roughly fifteen pairs of fixed ones. In a corner of a bedroom, that is the difference between having a gym and not having one.

<Ad />

Cost follows the same shape. A 2–24 kg adjustable pair lands well under what the equivalent fixed set costs, and it fits under a desk.

## Where adjustable loses

Dial-a-weight mechanisms take five to fifteen seconds to change. That sounds trivial until you run a drop set, or superset presses with rows. The rest interval you planned quietly becomes twice as long, and the training effect changes with it.

<Callout type="warn">
Selectorised dumbbells are not drop-safe. Training presses to failure without a rack means you will eventually drop them, and the plastic selector housing is what breaks.
</Callout>

## How to actually decide

- **Under 4 m² of space** → adjustable, no debate.
- **Training mostly strength, low reps, long rests** → adjustable works fine.
- **Metabolic work, supersets, drop sets** → buy fixed dumbbells in the two or three weights you actually use.
- **Both** → adjustable pair plus one fixed pair at your most-used weight. That covers 90% of sessions.

## The setup most people end up with

One adjustable pair, plus a fixed pair at the weight you press for sets of ten. The fixed pair takes the abuse and the fast transitions; the adjustable pair covers everything else.
