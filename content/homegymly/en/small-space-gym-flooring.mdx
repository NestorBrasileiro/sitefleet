Flooring is the one purchase people redo, because they buy for comfort and then start dropping weight.

## Match the floor to the load

- **Bodyweight, yoga, light dumbbells** → 12 mm foam puzzle mats. Cheap, warm, fine.
- **Dumbbells up to 30 kg, kettlebells** → 8 mm rubber tiles. They stay put and absorb a dropped bell.
- **Barbell, deadlifts** → 20 mm horse stall mats under the bar path. Nothing else takes repeated impact without cratering.

## The part everyone skips

Rubber needs to off-gas. New mats smell strongly for one to three weeks, and in a closed room that is genuinely unpleasant. Unroll them outdoors or in a garage for a few days before installing.

<Callout>
Measure the bar path, not the room. You need protection where the plates land, which is a strip roughly 2.2 m wide and 1 m deep. Covering the whole floor is a comfort choice, not a structural one.
</Callout>

## On upper floors

If you are above ground level, dropping a loaded barbell transmits through the slab to your neighbours regardless of mat thickness. The fix is not thicker rubber, it is lowering the bar under control, or using a deadlift platform with a plywood sandwich.
