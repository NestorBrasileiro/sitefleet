Doneness is temperature. Time on the grill is a consequence of thickness, fire and starting temperature — three things that change every cook.

## The numbers that matter

Centre temperature, probe-measured, **before** resting:

- **Rare** — 50 to 52 °C
- **Medium rare** — 55 °C
- **Medium** — 58 to 60 °C
- **Medium well** — 65 °C

<Ad />

Meat keeps climbing 3 to 5 °C after it leaves the heat. Pulling at 58 °C aiming for medium gives you 62 °C on the plate — overcooked.

## Start with the fat cap

Fat cap down, medium heat, and let it render. Fat needs time and moderate temperature to melt and brown; high heat scorches the cap before the meat cooks.

<Callout type="warn">
Piercing the meat with a fork to turn it costs you juice. Use tongs. The same applies to probing more often than you need to.
</Callout>

## Resting is not optional

Five to ten minutes off the heat, not tightly wrapped in foil — wrapping keeps it cooking and softens the crust you just built. The juices redistribute instead of running onto the board at first cut.

## Cut against the grain

Find the grain direction before you cut, not after. In picanha the fibres change direction near the tip, so it is worth rotating the cut halfway through.
