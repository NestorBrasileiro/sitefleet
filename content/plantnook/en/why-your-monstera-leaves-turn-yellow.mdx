Nine times out of ten, a yellow monstera leaf means the roots sat in water too long. The other causes look different, and telling them apart takes about thirty seconds.

## Read the pattern, not the colour

- **Oldest leaves yellow first, soil damp** → overwatering. The most common by far.
- **Yellow with crispy brown edges** → underwatering, or very low humidity.
- **Uniform pale yellow across new growth** → nitrogen shortage. It has been in the same soil for two years.
- **Yellow spots with a dark centre** → fungal, usually from water sitting on leaves.
- **One leaf, plant otherwise fine** → normal ageing. Ignore it.

## Fixing the common case

Stop watering. Lift the pot: if it feels heavy, the soil is still holding water. Wait until the top 5 cm is dry to a finger pushed in, then water thoroughly until it runs out the drainage hole.

<Callout type="warn">
A pot with no drainage hole guarantees this problem eventually. Decorative pots are fine as outer covers, but the plant must sit in a nursery pot that drains.
</Callout>

## When it is root rot

If the soil smells sour and the base of the stem is soft, it has gone past overwatering. Take the plant out, cut away black mushy roots with clean scissors, and repot in fresh chunky mix. Firm white roots stay.

## What not to do

Do not fertilise a yellowing plant that you suspect is overwatered. Damaged roots cannot take up nutrients, and the salt buildup makes it worse.
