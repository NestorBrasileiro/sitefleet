"Low light" in most plant lists means "bright indirect light, just not a south window". That is not the same as a hallway.

## The test

Hold your hand 30 cm above the spot at midday. If it casts no visible shadow, that is genuine low light, and the list narrows fast.

## The five that hold up

- **ZZ plant** — the strongest of the group. Survives months of neglect and near-darkness.
- **Snake plant** — slow in low light but does not sulk. Water very rarely.
- **Pothos** — will grow, though variegation fades to plain green.
- **Cast iron plant** — named for a reason, and genuinely hard to kill.
- **Philodendron hederaceum** — vines happily in dim corners.

<Callout>
Low light changes watering more than it changes the plant. Less light means slower growth means the soil stays wet longer. Halve your watering frequency compared to a bright spot, or you will rot the roots you were trying to protect.
</Callout>

## What to skip

Fiddle leaf figs, calatheas, most ferns and anything sold as "variegated" will decline in true low light. They tolerate it for a season and then thin out.
